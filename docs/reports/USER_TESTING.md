# Wikiplotter User Testing 

## Tasks 

| Task | Instruction | Rationale | 
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Begin exploring a hub. | Find the Avatar: The Last Airbender wiki, set your progress to Book 1, Episode 1, and explore the articles available to you on the first episode. | Evaluate whether hub discovery, onboarding, and browsing are intuitive without guidance. | 
| 2. Update your reading progress. | Pretend you've binged until Book 1, Episode 10. Update your progress and find out information about a character that wasn't there before. | Evaluate how intuitive it is to set reading progress and evaluate user's understanding of its purpose. |
| 3. Register for an account. | Create an account and verify that your reading progress is saved from browsing anonymously. | Evaluate how smooth the account onboarding process is and test progress synchronization. |
| 4. Edit an article. | Visit the "User Testing" hub, find the "Test Four" article, and edit it to update the quoted text to a new kaomoji. Verify that the changes are saved after editing. | Test the ease of the article editing experience and evaluate users' understanding of the article view. | 
| 5. Start your own hub. | Go back to the homepage, and start a new hub with your name of choice. Create up to ten checkpoints with their own names. Create an empty first article with your title of choice, then upload three images with captions and a sentence block explaining each image. Set appropriate checkpoints. | Evaluate the hub management experience and evaluate users' understanding of the application's features. | 
| 6. Manage your data. | Find the settings page, and update your username. Find your uploaded media in the application, and delete one of your images. Upload a new one directly from the Uploads page. Finally, log out of Wikiplotter. | Test the ease of navigation for settings and data, and evaluate users' understanding of their data in the application. | 

## Reports 

### Participant A 

This participant was familiar with reading fictional stories and browsing the web, but not necessarily using online wiki platforms. The participant quickly moved through the initial navigation, complimenting the clean and minimalistic UI. However, they commented that updating their reading progress felt a little inaccessible, hidden behind a button. Upon reaching the hub creation step, they commented on the redundancy of the "Welcome" message, requiring the owner of the hub itself to set their own progress. They also expressed dissatisfaction with hub management, specifically adding checkpoints, reporting the redundancy of the warning messages. Perhaps due to this friction, they stopped at adding their fifth checkpoint. The participant commented that the hub management page could potentially be useful to organize articles by their checkpoints and be able to proceed to edit them. During the article editing task, the participant reported no problems writing simple sentences and uploading images, but took extra note of the save button not requiring a confirmation warning before certain edge cases, like changing an image's alt text. During the last task, the participant easily found the settings page, but took a few seconds to find the page for media under the data tab. They also took note of unclear reasons provided when their intended username was rejected by the system. Overall, the participant exhibited proficiency in navigating the application, appreciating the ease of navigation and robust functionality of current features, while taking note of various discrepancies between the application and their expectations.

### Participant B

This participant was already familiar with browsing with an account on online wikis and uploading their own media, but hadn't seen checkpointing before. The participant was able to navigate the homepage and the Avatar wiki quickly, showing familiarity with normal wiki tools. They noted that the Aang article was noticeably more empty than normal wikis, potentially assuming it was simply incomplete rather than that content was hidden from them. They expressed particular satisfaction upon seeing more of the article after updating their progress to Episode 10. The participant needed to take a couple of seconds to find the button leading that allowed them to update their progress. There was friction during the article editing task, where the participant was able to find the test article immediately, but became confused at not being able to find the target block, likely due to unfamiliarity with the spoiler filtering yet. They eventually found the target block after deciding to click the edit button on the article, and expressed surprise at the amount of text editing tools, followed by subsequent disappointment that they were non-functional. The participant also reported friction with the text editing features, with the text area not allowing for easy cursor navigation or highlighting. They expressed appreciation for the re-ordering features, as well as skepticism about the application for the fact that every block becomes a paragraph, comparing it in contrast to normal Fandom wiki layouts. During the hub creation process, the participant also expressed frustration with the process of manually setting individual checkpoints, but otherwise complimented the breadth of hub management features. There was no reported difficulty with the remaining account-related tasks. Overall, the participant expressed favorability towards the idea of checkpointing wikis, but emphasizes the current Wikiplotter's text editing to be a major blocker dissuading them from using the application. 

## Conclusions

Overall, both participants were able to get through the designated tasks without requiring intervention or additional guidance, while reporting varying degrees of difficulty and friction for each task. The following are potential issues coupled with potential room for improvement: 

| Degree/Severity | Issue Description | Potential Causes | Potential Improvement | 
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical/Major | 1. Clunky article-editing experience. | From a broken text box to missing text-editing features, users are currently forced to put up with a primitive version of a text-editor, dissuading any extensive article customization. | Improving the text area element, as well as adding rich text editing features. In the future, a text-editing markup language/syntax could also make the experience smoother. |
| Physical/Moderate | 2. Tedious checkpoint management experience. | Redundant warning messages and tedious one-by-one controls for adding checkpoints can decentivize the use of checkpoints, especially for stories that might have hundreds of chapters. | In the future, bulk checkpoint-adding can solve both the redundancy and tediousness issues. Future controls for managing a long checkpoint list, such as search or multi-line edits, are also possible. |
| Conceptual/Moderate | 3. Unclear hub access roles. | Without contributor roles and hub rule customization, it can be hard to understand the point of the current access controls for the hub creator or the point of the wiki platform itself. | In the future, having these rules and roles created at the creation of a hub can help solve the issue.  |
| Physical/Minor | 4. Trouble navigating buttons. | The current buttons may be placed in awkward positions, with ambiguous icons, or it might be unclear if they are buttons at all. | In the future, buttons can be reworked. As application functionality expands, it could also make sense to group functionality buttons under an expandable, universal button icon (e.g. kebab/meatball menu) which can be more recognizable for users. |
| Conceptual/Minor | 5. Lack of onboarding for spoiler filtering. | Current hubs are treated like articles to browse, without explanation of the features of Wikiplotter itself, making it potentially confusing the intended usage of the platform or what it provides. | Having a specialized UI, coupled with onboarding or initiation to Wikiplotter itself, on the home page to distinguish the platform itself conceptually. |

To conclude this user testing phase, Wikiplotter's user design is still very much in beta, with many features, ideas, and considerations left to be implemented.
